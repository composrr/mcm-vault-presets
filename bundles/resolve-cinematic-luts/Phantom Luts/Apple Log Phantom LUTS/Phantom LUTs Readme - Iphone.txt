This Readme is designed to help you get started.

One key idea I tried to infuse into the LUTs was the idea of simplicity and convenience - however, the main goal was obviously to make LUTs look great - but let’s talk more about the idea of convenience.

I like efficiency and prefer products and services that just ‘work’ without tweaking and effort - that’s the earmark of great design in my mind. Using LOG can be confusing and daunting at first. These principles of convenience and simplicity underlie every decision I made in the years I spent developing these LUTs over many different iterations (and the developments continue).

The 65 Point Cube retains more information and is usually used for heavy files 4K and higher. If you're working with 1080p files, a 33-Point Cube should be adequate.

1) What Is the Difference Between the ‘Standard Exposure’ and ‘Legacy’ Versions of the LUTs?
Both versions have the same color; The difference lies in the gamma curves (contrast) and Exposure Values (EV). Each version is intended to be used all along the workflow chain. So you should shoot/monitor on set with the version you plan on grading with - as they’ll cause you to expose differently, and grading is much faster and simpler when you nail exposure in camera on set.

- Standard Versions

In most scenarios, I’d recommend using the standard versions as they are optimized for the iPhone sensors and their noise floor to offer a great balance between a very natural contrast and latitude. These LUTs also have the same exposure value as the Conversion LUTs, albeit with slightly different contrast. So if you don’t have a monitor or left it at home, then you’ll be able to use the Gamma Display Assist function to get exposure that will work with the Standard Versions later in post. These look best with normally exposed footage - in my opinion, you don’t need to over-expose with these new iPhone sensors - they’re very clean regarding noise and artifacts.
If you’re trying to match your iPhone with an Arri camera, I recommend the standard version of the Neutral LUT, which directly emulates the Alexa709 LUT in terms of color and contrast.

- Legacy Versions

The Legacy versions are a little darker and have slightly more contrast and lifted blacks. They also have an exposure compensation built in of just under one stop - so they will cause you to over expose about a stop most times for a natural looking image. The Legacy versions have the same gamma curve as the older Fs7 LUTs but with the iPhone colour. So as an example, let’s say you are shooting on an Fs7 and a C70 together on a shoot. If you use the Fs7 Neutral LUT for example, you’d want to be using the C70 version of the Neutral LUT and in particular the ‘Legacy’ version of the Neutral to end up with the closest match. That’s their main purpose - to make backwards compatibility easy.

Another reason you might want to use the Legacy versions is because they should help get cleaner footage in lowlight scenarios if you’re forced to use higher ISOs. This is because, as previously stated they will tend to get you to over expose more than the standard versions and also the gamma curve was developed over years for the older Sony sensors (which were much noisier) to help clean up noise while still preserving shadow detail. So my advice is, if you’re often shooting in lowlight scenarios with high ISOs (not recommended but sometimes the circumstances call for it) then do some tests comparing the standard and Legacy versions to see which you’d prefer to use. Though remember that 50% of the LUTs job is helping you expose on set, so for best results, I strongly recommend you shoot with the same version you plan on grading with to make life easier for yourself in post.

2) WDR Pre-LUT
For those who want just that little extra bit out of the dynamic range, I've included WDR (Wide Dynamic Range) Pre-LUTs. The idea is you apply them before whichever LUT (the same way you do Colour Correction) you want to use and it will retain more highlight and shadow information and give a general Low-Contrast look; though it does come at the expense of highlight rolloff so keep that in mind. Clipping highlights are less eye-catching with the highlight rolloff in the regular LUTs
but that comes at the expense of highlight information. There are pros and cons to both - but options are always good.

A lot of iPhone users have emailed me describing their dissatisfaction with the colors coming out of its camera using the native LUT. More LUTs at Freevideoeffect.com.  Having recently purchased an iPhone 15 myself, I decided to develop a LUT to emulate my Alexa mini using the tried and true process I developed with Sony and Blackmagic cameras. While no camera is perfect, personally, I find Alexa’s color science to be the gold standard in terms of natural, accurate, and beautiful colors right out of the box as a starting point to grade from.

Over a few days, I shot exposure-matched test footage with my Alexa mini and my iPhone side by side with matched. I shot in sunlight, shadow, overcast, indoor daylight, indoor mixed lighting, controlled studio lighting and all with my trusty Xrite Colour Checker Classic Card and my Xrite Digital Color Checker SG Card. 

In total I shot approximately 80-100 scenes this way. With each set up I recorded 3 exposures - under 2 stops over 2 stops and correct exposure. With the extensive data gathered, I was able to build an emulation that is extremely accurate; factoring in not just the overall color response but also the more subtle aspects of the Alexa color science that it borrowed from a photochemical film such as it’s nonlinear hue responses for rich, complex colors, subtractive color modeling in relation to luminance vs saturation dynamics and Alexa’s overall saturation profile for beautiful highlight roll off and shadow falloff to name a few.

I’m really happy with the emulation and hope you enjoy it!

The 5 LUTs Described Below:

LUT 1) Neutral - Rebuild of the Alexa Classic 709 LUT
* High quality recreation of the legacy Arri Alexa colour science found in the first generation of Alexa cameras using the ALEV III sensor.
LUT 2) Tungsten - A slightly warmer golden cast designed to be utilised for subjects bathed in tungsten light. 
LUT 3) Ice Blue - Blue, colder cast with punchy, deep blues (looks amazing on subjects with blue eyes).
LUT 4) Jamaica - Green and gold cast - distinctive, rich, filmic greens and beautiful golden skintones.
LUT 5) Utopia - the most filmic look of the LUTs, with blood red reds and filmic blues and greens. It’s my personal take on what Utopia would look like. 

The ‘Film Look’ LUT Pack contains 6 LUTs inspired by real film stocks designed to give your Apple Log Footage the Film Look. The emulations are based on my own research and subjective impressions of the film stocks listed.

The 5 LUTs Described Below:

LUT 1) ‘Vision’ - Modelled on Kodak Vision 5274 

Shot on: The Matrix, Lord of the Rings Trilogy, Gladiator
The most colour accurate stock with accurate blues, slightly cool greens, accurate skin tones which slightly skew pink/gold
Strong contrast and filmic subtractive colour model in saturation
Gentle roll off and rich, smooth fall off in shadows
Skews green in shadow areas and when underexposed
Classic film character but less stylised than any other stock

LUT 2) ‘Eastman’ - Modelled on Kodak Eastman 5247

Shot on: Apocalypse Now, Bladerunner, Alien, ET
Vintage, dated Kodak look and feel.
Saturation strongest in mids and falls off in shadows and highlights.
Greens skew warm, blues slightly turquoise, gold/pink skin-tones.
Smoother contrast than modern stocks.

LUT 3) ‘Eastman RM’ - Variation on Kodak Eastman 5247 (Introduced Jan 2022)

Classic Eastman Look but modernised (Orange & Teal)
Greener in cast than the more magenta original Eastman 5247
Modelled on the Re-Graded versions of classic films from 70s & 80s

LUT 4) ‘Vision Teal’ -  Variation on Kodak Vision 5274 (LUT# 1)

Subtle Orange/Teal colour scheme.
More stylised version of ‘#1 Vision’. Less realistic - ‘prettier’.
Greens are slightly warmer, blues slightly turquoise and skin more golden.
Overall lift in saturation and contrast compared to ‘Vision’ but still balanced.
Can elevate a dull scene with its look but not overpower a rich scene.
LUT 5) ‘Eterna’ - Modelled on Fuji Eterna 8563 

Shot on: Captain Phillips, Wall Street: Money Never Sleeps, The Butler
Overall smoother contrast and rolloff compared to Kodak.
Rich but not overpowering colours and saturation characteristics.
My go-to for Fashion and Beauty but can work with almost anything.
Very flattering skin-tones.
Greens are cool and rich, skin is golden, blues skew turquoise and modern, colour accurate yellows compared to Kodak.

LUT 6) ‘Bleach’ - Modelled on Fujifilm Super F 8552 

Shot on: Munich, War of The Worlds, Crash
Modelling the Bleach Bypass process for strong contrast and low saturation.
Aggressive, gritty look - halfway between black and white and colour film.
Great for War Films, Crime, Urban Fashion.
Works especially well with heavy film grain.
